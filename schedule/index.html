Format

Besides the talks, the day has three parts.

  • The claims board. Two months before the workshop, the organizers and speakers will collect about twenty empirical claims about AI that current policy documents, system cards or projections rely on, each with its source. Every submission marks the claims it bears on. At the workshop, each claim gets a physical board next to the posters it concerns, and attendees post evidence for and against it there. The closing session reports which claims gained support and which lost it, and we will publish the board on this website afterwards.
  • A panel with a working question: which claims about AI is society already relying on, and which of them would we stake a decision on today? An organizer moderates, and the panelists come from theory, evaluation, and algorithms and society.
  • Re-analysis spotlights. One oral session is reserved for tiny papers, so a three-page re-test of a widely cited number gets the same stage as a full paper.

If you cannot attend

With the speakers' consent, we will record the talks and the panel and post them on this website. Accepted papers and their posters will be public on OpenReview by February 25, 2027. If you are an author and cannot travel because of a visa or another constraint, you can send a five-minute video and a poster PDF instead. We will display your poster on site and post your video here. After the workshop, we will publish the claims board, with the evidence attached to each claim, as a short report.

ICLR gives each workshop four complimentary registrations, and we will use ours to support junior and underrepresented attendees. We will also look for outside sponsors to fund travel grants for them.
